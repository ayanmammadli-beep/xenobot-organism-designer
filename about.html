Overview

Building Infrastructure for Programmable Biology

Biology is rapidly emerging as the next great engineering platform. MorphoCapture addresses a fundamental barrier in this transition: the inability to systematically design and develop living machines at scale.

Our platform transforms xenobot development from a manual, specialized process into an accessible, AI-driven workflow. By combining evolutionary algorithms, physics-based simulation, and automated protocol generation, we enable researchers to design functional biological structures without requiring specialized computational infrastructure or extensive manual fabrication expertise.

This approach unlocks applications across multiple domains including targeted drug delivery, environmental remediation, tissue engineering, and cellular-scale manufacturing.

Background

Scientific Foundation

In 2020, researchers at Tufts University and the University of Vermont demonstrated that stem cells from Xenopus laevis embryos, when isolated and placed in culture, spontaneously self-organize into novel multicellular structures with emergent behaviors. These xenobots exhibited locomotion, collective behavior, and self-repair capabilities without genetic modification.

Design Methodology

The original design process utilized evolutionary algorithms running on supercomputing infrastructure. The system simulated thousands of cellular configurations, evaluating each for functional criteria such as directed movement or object manipulation. Successful designs were then manually assembled using micromanipulation techniques.

Key Findings

Subsequent research in 2021 demonstrated kinematic replication capabilities. Xenobots could aggregate dispersed cells and compress them into new functional structures, representing the first instance of AI-designed biological systems exhibiting reproductive behavior. This validated the potential for programmable living machines.

Problem Statement

Current Limitations

Despite their demonstrated potential across biomedical, environmental, and manufacturing applications, xenobots remain constrained to a small number of specialized research facilities. This limitation stems from systemic barriers in the design and fabrication pipeline.

Technical Barriers

Design requires access to high-performance computing clusters for evolutionary simulation. Fabrication demands specialized micromanipulation equipment and expertise. The end-to-end process is time-intensive, requiring weeks to months per design iteration.

Access Barriers

Most research institutions lack the computational infrastructure, fabrication facilities, and cross-disciplinary expertise required. This creates a significant bottleneck in translating xenobot technology from fundamental research to practical application across medicine, environmental science, and biotechnology.

Platform

MorphoCapture System Architecture

Automated Design-to-Protocol Pipeline

MorphoCapture provides an end-to-end platform for xenobot development. Researchers specify functional objectives through a structured interface. The system employs evolutionary algorithms and physics-based simulation to generate optimized cellular morphologies, validates designs through virtual testing, and produces detailed fabrication protocols.

The platform architecture consists of three integrated components: a generative design engine utilizing evolutionary computation, a soft-body physics simulator for behavioral prediction, and an automated protocol generator that translates digital designs into laboratory procedures. This eliminates the need for specialized computational infrastructure while maintaining scientific rigor.

Iterative Refinement

Experimental results from fabricated designs feed back into the optimization pipeline. This closed-loop approach enables continuous model improvement and builds a comprehensive dataset of structure-function relationships in biological machines.

Research Accessibility

By abstracting computational complexity and providing standardized protocols, MorphoCapture makes xenobot development accessible to any laboratory with basic cell culture capabilities. This democratization accelerates research and enables broader application development.

Applications

Target Domains

Xenobot technology enables applications that require biocompatibility, biodegradability, and autonomous behavior at cellular scales. MorphoCapture facilitates rapid prototyping and optimization for domain-specific requirements.

Biomedical

Targeted drug delivery to specific tissues, plaque removal from arterial walls, minimally invasive surgical assistance, and cellular-level diagnostics. Biocompatibility and biodegradability eliminate concerns about long-term implant complications.

Environmental

Microplastic remediation in aquatic environments, toxin detection and neutralization, localized pollutant sequestration, and ecosystem monitoring at micro scales. Self-biodegradation prevents secondary contamination.

Tissue Engineering

Scaffold-free tissue construction, vascular network formation, cellular organization and patterning, and developmental biology research. Living machines can guide and facilitate complex tissue assembly processes.

Research Tools

Experimental platforms for developmental biology, cellular mechanics studies, morphogenesis investigation, and testing hypotheses about collective cellular behavior and self-organization principles.
